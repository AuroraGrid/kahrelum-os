How to interpret the public election files

The directory indexes research source records, amendments, competition locks and data snapshots. These files have different roles. A frozen forecast records what an analyst or external comparator believed at the issued cutoff; a subsequent observation describes incoming evidence; a result file may report unofficial race calls; a separately dated official certificate can settle only the outcome defined in that competition's registered rules. None of these labels should be collapsed into a single claim of predictive performance.

For U.S. election material, live counts and source-provided calls remain unofficial until the relevant state certification. A map snapshot contains its source retrieval time and should not be treated as a continuously refreshed national certified result. For Israel, the C7 calibration covers the externally attributed October 26 comparator against the November 4 official result, with the outcome recorded on November 5. It is not evidence that KAHRELUM issued the comparator forecast itself.

Public data tables, date-stamped corrections and exclusions remain visible even when a result is unfavourable. The listed source evidence does not authorize private record collection or retrospective modification of locked predictions. See the source glossary, forecasting desk, and Israel C7 outcome audit for the relevant definitions.

Before citing a historical election record

Begin with the file's named jurisdiction, issuer, source timestamp and purpose. An administrative release, a data relay and a model projection can all contain useful numbers, but they are different evidence objects. A public data file is not, by itself, a definitive statement about an election's certification, governance outcome or future policy. Where a registration or certification standard applies, prefer the specific legal authority and document that meet that standard rather than a media excerpt or an earlier draft.

Original forecast locks must remain unchanged when new vote counts, candidate withdrawals or official results arrive. Any later error calculation should identify its comparison set, the exact cutoff and whether a forecast was independent, externally attributed, retrospective or post-close. A favorable result obtained by altering its benchmark after the outcome would not establish prospective skill. An unfavorable result should remain available alongside its source limitations, without being silently withheld from readers.

KAHRELUM's public pages are research and educational documentation, not a voting service, official certification platform, accredited election audit or legal authority. Unassessed states, unresolved source conflicts and missing original records should stay identified as unknown until additional primary evidence is available. For corrections, cite the specific public record and the associated date, then preserve the prior version in the audit trail.
