Three Brier calculations that still do not count.

Marvin Harrison Jr., Quenton Nelson and DeForest Buckner were traded. KAHRELUM can display the mechanical error for each recovered percentage. It still cannot call those rows a certified forecasting result.

October 31, 2026 · Based on the append-only public forecast ledger

The arithmetic

For a binary event, the Brier score is the squared distance between a probability p and an outcome y, where a completed trade is 1 and no trade is 0.

Brier = (p − y)²

Lower is better, but only on an eligible record. A perfect 1.00 forecast on a trade that occurred scores 0. A 0.50 forecast scores 0.25. The formula cannot establish when a forecast was actually made.

Why none of the three enters the certified score

The percentages were recovered from prior conversations, but the original prospective publication timestamps were not independently authenticated. That leaves the most important question unresolved: was the exact probability locked before the relevant outcome and before outcome-revealing information?

A valid scoring ledger needs the exact event definition, deadline, information cutoff, public timestamp, evidence available at cutoff, immutable commit reference, and later outcome. Without that custody, showing the calculation is useful transparency; aggregating it into a performance claim would be hindsight contamination.

Current certified NFL denominator: zero. There are no independently resolved, timestamp-qualified prospective locks in the current deadline audit. The certified mean Brier score therefore remains N/A—not the average of these three rows.

What happens next

Future eligible forecasts are append-only. A revision receives its own timestamp and record hash; it does not overwrite the original. After the deadline, the audit must include traded and non-traded players, correct and incorrect calls, a baseline, and every eligible record—not only headline successes.
