Election intelligence · methods

Keep the monitor alive. Keep its lanes separate.

A page can refresh every minute and still mislead if it collapses official counting, secondary relay data, count-derived seat arithmetic and model forecasts into one number.

October 31, 2026 · Neutral methods note; no new outcome or government-formation claim

Four lanes, four questions

1 · Official count

What has the election authority published?

Identity, timestamp, units, party coverage, ballot arithmetic and denominator semantics must all be checked before admission.

2 · Secondary relay

What has another outlet reproduced?

A relay can improve access, but it is not automatically the legal result or independent corroboration. Capture time and source update time can differ.

3 · Count-derived seats

What allocation follows from the votes currently visible?

A partial-count allocation describes the counted subset. It is not an unbiased final forecast unless the remaining-vote process is modeled and validated.

4 · KAHRELUM forecast

What does the model estimate from a stated cutoff?

Exit-informed and post-close model runs must be labeled as such. They cannot be backdated into pre-election forecasts or mixed with C0 custody.

“Live” is a provenance claim

A healthy monitor must disclose its last successful source check, last published update, source stage and freshness. A failed retrieval should produce a visible stale state, not a recycled timestamp that looks current.

KAHRELUM preserves raw hashes and append-only review evidence. A retired CSV stays retired from public pages; it is not silently re-imported to fill a source gap. Frozen predictions and original scoring rules remain frozen even when later data is more complete.

Election remains active. The public Election Lab and monitor routes remain part of the production regression set. Sports publishing must not replace or archive them.

What the public should be able to inspect

  • The primary source and the exact retrieval time.
  • Whether a number is votes, ballot boxes, registered voters, turnout, seats or a model output.
  • The stage of the count and whether late or special ballots remain.
  • The difference between a source’s displayed update time and KAHRELUM’s capture time.
  • The forecast issue time, information cutoff, revision history and scoring eligibility.
  • Conflicts between sources without averaging the disagreement away.
