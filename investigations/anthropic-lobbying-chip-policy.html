Anthropic’s lobbying, Rubio connections and the evidence gap around Jensen Huang

Evidence reviewed November 17, 2026. Original records and clearly labeled secondary reproductions; coordination remains unproven.

Federal records establish paid representation and professional connections. They do not establish that Anthropic orchestrated Jensen Huang’s departure from Nvidia.

What the money establishes

The official federal lobbying register lists the following income reported by Continental Strategy and Ballard Partners for Anthropic. KAHRELUM independently recalculated the totals from that register. These are lobbying-income estimates, not audited bank payments, personal payments to officials or evidence of a payment for a particular government decision.

The combined figure is $1.63 million across different reporting periods. A dash means no amount is included for Ballard in this series, not that Anthropic had no other lobbying expenses. These two firms are not Anthropic’s entire lobbying operation. Adding outside-firm income to Anthropic’s separately reported expenses risks counting overlapping spending twice.

The inspected Continental Q1 2026 form describes its income as a good-faith estimate rounded to the nearest $10,000. All nine amounts match the official index previously inspected and the filing-level Open America reproductions reviewed in this follow-up. The eight previously blocked original quarterly bodies were not inspected: their personnel, agency and issue descriptions below are attributed to secondary reproductions. A known omission prevents treating those reproductions as guaranteed exhaustive transcripts.

A real Rubio connection, with a specific evidentiary limit

Continental’s registration was effective April 24, 2025, and posted May 7, 2025. It identifies Alberto Martinez’s prior position as chief of staff to Senator Marco Rubio. It lists Martinez and Paul Calkins Jr. and describes an AI-development and national-security assignment.

The Q1 2026 Continental report reports $150,000 and lists Martinez, Paul Calkins and Carlos Trujillo. It identifies Congress and the Executive Office of the President as contacted institutions. It does not identify a conversation with Rubio about Huang, a client instruction to seek Huang’s departure, or any quid pro quo.

This distinction matters: a former government role establishes a professional connection. It does not establish that the former official obtained a particular outcome from that office.

Ballard’s scope changed from procurement to U.S. AI policy

Ballard’s registration was effective March 9, 2026, but signed and posted April 10, 2026. Filing date and engagement-effective date must not be conflated.

The form names Brian Ballard, Sylvester Lukis and Micah Ketchel. Ketchel disclosed State Department and White House advisory positions in 2025 and prior service as Mike Waltz’s congressional chief of staff. The registration describes defense procurement. Open America reproduces that scope for Q1 and Q2, but Q3 changes to U.S. policy regarding AI. The Q3 reproduction names Micah Ketchel and Amaryllis Fox Kennedy and lists seven government bodies. This broader scope is material; it still does not establish an assignment to remove Huang or justify allocating the entire fee specifically to chip-export lobbying.

The Q3 reproduction discloses Kennedy’s former intelligence and White House-related positions. These are professional-background disclosures, not evidence of misconduct. This investigation makes no claim about unverified family connections or participation by other people employed at either firm.

A documented policy position, not proof of a personnel operation

Anthropic’s own policy statement, read directly in the browser, advocates strengthening semiconductor export controls. It proposes adjusting the tier system through government agreements, lowering the no-license threshold for Tier 2 countries and increasing enforcement resources. It frames those recommendations as protecting national security, economic prosperity and domestic AI infrastructure. These are Anthropic’s stated reasons, not an independent finding about its private motives. We do not rely on the inaccessible PDF for an H20-specific claim.

An independently downloaded Federal Register rule, document 2026-00789, establishes a separate policy milestone. Effective January 15, 2026, BIS changed the review policy for qualifying H200 and equivalent exports to China and Macau from a presumption of denial to case-by-case consideration, subject to conditions. This was not an unrestricted export authorization. The January 13 announcement and January 15 effective date are distinct. The historical rule alone does not establish the entire policy in force in November.

Security arguments and commercial incentives can coexist. An AI developer may favor tighter controls while having business interests affected by them; a chip seller may favor overseas market access while arguing that American technology leadership benefits security. Those possibilities do not identify the cause of an executive’s departure.

The Commerce–Justice statement on Megaspeed places the investigation’s origin before both registrations. That weighs against the claim that either engagement initiated this particular investigation. It does not resolve every possible later-influence claim. The statement establishes the government’s account, not criminal guilt.

Nvidia’s original SEC Form 8-K, Item 5.02 records the board’s acceptance of Huang’s resignation as president, CEO and director on November 11, effective immediately. Kress became interim CEO while retaining her CFO role. The November 12 company announcement corroborates the transition. Reuters’ November 12 report, read in full through its syndication, also reports the departure and Kress appointment and describes Huang’s push to preserve access to China’s market. We rely on the SEC filing for the precise effective date.

A recorded resignation does not by itself resolve whether the decision was voluntary or pressured. None of these sources establishes an Anthropic-directed operation. Unauthenticated classified-memo allegations and their purported date have been removed from this investigation; no claim about removal from a travel delegation is made.

Limitations and correction policy

This is an open-source documentary review, without access to confidential communications, intelligence records or board deliberations. No interviews or requests for comment were conducted; no party is described as having declined to respond. Private-life and espionage allegations are not authenticated by repetition and are unnecessary to establish the lobbying figures.

The follow-up recovered filing-level secondary reproductions and directly inspected the policy statement and original SEC/issuer records.
