FAA nationwide ground stop: communications disruption, initiating failure undetermined

AIR-02 · OPEN · Evidence review cutoff: November 13, 2026, 6:00 p.m. Eastern (23:00 UTC)

This is a dated evidence snapshot. It does not revise or extend AIR-01 or the prior 12-record portfolio snapshot.

Editorial illustration: original AIR-02 artwork, not an official FAA image.

Question and disposition

What is established about the November 13 nationwide ground stop, and what evidence identifies the initiating technical failure?

Disposition: OPEN. Cause UNKNOWN. The ground stop is confirmed. Contemporaneous reporting attributed it to communications problems affecting air route traffic control centers. The specific failed equipment, network or service, failure sequence, duration by component, and any cybersecurity involvement were not verified in the reviewed sources at the stated cutoff.

Evidence findings

FACT — Nationwide departure ground stop

The FAA issued a nationwide ground stop for departing flights on November 13, citing extraordinary circumstances and safety. The precise operational boundaries, exceptions and lift time should be read from the dated FAA notices; reporting may summarize them differently.

Reuters, November 13, 2026, FAA issues national ground stop

REPORTED — Communications problems at regional centers

AP reported that Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy described a communications issue affecting the 22 regional air route traffic control centers. This is an attributed public statement, not a technical incident report identifying the failed component.

Associated Press, November 13, 2026, initial report

AP's later update reported the FAA ground stop and continuing investigation. It does not establish a root cause.

Associated Press, November 13, 2026, later update

UNKNOWN — Initiating technical failure

The public material reviewed did not identify a verified hardware, software, telecommunications provider, configuration change, or common dependency as the initiating cause. A broad report that communications were affected does not by itself prove every center or every communications path failed in the same way.

UNKNOWN — Cybersecurity involvement

No evidence reviewed established a cyberattack or ruled one out. No cybersecurity explanation is asserted as fact.

Historical context, kept separate from cause

AP compared this event with the January 2023 NOTAM system outage and described it as the first nationwide ground stop since that event. The FAA's 2023 review attributed that earlier NOTAM disruption to files unintentionally deleted during database synchronization work. That history does not establish that AIR-02 had the same cause or involved the NOTAM system.

FAA, review of the January 2023 NOTAM outage

The September 11, 2001 national airspace shutdown followed terrorist attacks and is a distinct national-security event. It is not evidence of a relationship to the 2026 communications issue.

Follow-up v1.1: the ground stop is lifted; the technical investigation remains open

Added November 13th, 2026. Evidence cutoff: 11:27 p.m. Eastern / November 14th, 04:27 UTC. This addendum extends the record. The original 6:00 p.m. Eastern snapshot above is preserved unchanged.

What changed in the operational record

The nationwide departure restriction has ended. FAA advisory 111 carries a November 13th event time of 20:53 UTC and an effective/signature time of 20:54 UTC, or 3:54 p.m. Eastern. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy's later timeline places the initial telecommunications problems at 3:36 p.m. and the lifting of the stop at 8:15 p.m. Eastern. These are different milestones, not interchangeable start times.

Cancellation advisory 005 records an event window beginning at 01:16 UTC on November 14th and an effective/signature time of 01:19 UTC, corresponding to 8:16 p.m. and 8:19 p.m. Eastern on November 13th. The notice confirms cancellation; its timestamps should not be silently substituted for Duffy's 8:15 p.m. announcement.

Duffy said more than 3,500 aircraft were airborne when the stop was issued and fewer than 400 remained airborne at 8:00 p.m. He also said the FAA did not lose all radar or telecommunications and allowed exceptions for critical medical, life-saving cargo and law-enforcement flights. These are attributed official operational figures, not counts of canceled flights. Ending a departure restriction does not establish that airline schedules, crews or passengers have fully recovered.

Explanation, evidence and cyber uncertainty

Duffy subsequently blamed aging analog equipment. ABC's November 13th briefing coverage describes officials' preliminary explanation of data overwhelming older equipment. It also reports Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin's qualified assessment: no indications of malicious cyber activity, while investigation continued. That assessment narrows the current public explanation; it does not constitute a published forensic exclusion.

The FAA's preliminary statement still describes communications problems and investigation. No final technical incident report was found in the material reviewed by this cutoff. The equipment-overload account is therefore an official preliminary explanation, not an independently demonstrated failure sequence. A failure involving older equipment does not, by itself, establish which new equipment, interface, configuration or provider initiated it.

Disruption numbers require their own clock

AP's indexed report, attributed to Cirium, counted 827 diversions as of 7:05 p.m. Eastern: 670 returns to origin and 157 landings elsewhere. Access to the full AP page failed during this review; this figure was available in its indexed text and remains attributed reporting. It is a time-bounded diversion tally, not 827 cancellations, stranded passengers or unique people. A comparable final cancellation and delay total was not independently established here; those totals are left open rather than combined with diversions.

Funding is not the same as expenditure

GAO's September 2026 modernization review records a $12.5 billion congressional appropriation in July 2025 and identifies gaps in comprehensive lifecycle cost estimates and an integrated schedule. An appropriation provides budget authority; it does not prove how much was paid, what was delivered or what equipment was operational on November 13th. Additional funding requests are proposals until enacted. The sources reviewed do not establish actual expenditure attributable to the affected components. Nor does GAO's earlier planning critique establish the cause of this outage.

Updated assessment and next evidence

AIR-02 remains OPEN. The stop's cancellation is documented. An equipment-related explanation is now publicly attributed to officials. The initiating component, failure sequence, scope by facility and independently verified cyber assessment remain unresolved. Useful next evidence includes the FAA incident report, time-stamped system and configuration records, provider findings, restoration tests, an attributable forensic assessment, and contracts, obligations, outlays and acceptance records for the affected modernization work.
