Investigations / CHIP-01 / OPEN

Nvidia, Megaspeed and the AI-chip custody question

An open, source-linked investigation into corporate control, GPU shipments, U.S. export rules and reported counterintelligence concerns.

Original evidence reviewed November 8, 2026 · Updated November 10, 2026 · No finding of wrongdoing · Read the full Insight

Evidence addendum · November 10, 2026 update

New evidence: independent board review, litigation and the next decision gates

Source cutoff: November 10, 2026. This append-only editorial update follows the November 8 baseline; it does not re-date the original research or claim an earlier forecast lock. Nvidia's own board statement and Huang's own message establish new public facts. They do not establish that espionage occurred or that protected information was compromised.

PRIMARY · Nov 8 · Corporate governance

Nvidia announces independent inquiry

The independent directors, with outside counsel, are reviewing reporting that Huang may have been targeted by foreign intelligence. The board says it has no reason to believe national-security information or Nvidia secrets were compromised, and expresses confidence in his leadership. An inquiry is not a finding.

Official board statement

PRIMARY · Nov 8 · Direct admission

Huang publicly acknowledges infidelity

In a message published by Nvidia, Huang apologized to his family and colleagues and said he would cooperate with the board's review. He did not identify the other person in that statement. Personal misconduct alone is not proof of espionage, a leak or chip diversion; his family deserves privacy.

Read Huang's statement

PRIMARY · Nov 9 · Contrary government statement

China rejects the intelligence allegation

Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun called the reported spy allegation unsubstantiated and a smear against China. The response is a documented denial, not an independent audit of the underlying U.S. reporting.

Official Nov 9 briefing transcript

ATTRIBUTED · Nov 9 · Presidential remarks

Trump describes a conversation and an FBI inquiry

Trump said Huang told him he did not know the woman was a spy, and said the FBI was looking into the matter. Reuters reported those statements. This update has not independently authenticated an FBI announcement or its investigative scope.

Reuters on Trump's remarks

REPORTED FILING · Nov 9 · Civil litigation

Proposed shareholder class action

Reuters reports a complaint filed in federal court in San Jose by shareholders led by Mark Kistenmacher, alleging that Nvidia and Huang failed to disclose material risks. It is a class action allegation, not a judicial finding of securities fraud; no verified court merits decision is reported here.

Reuters filing report

OBSERVED · Nov 9 · Markets

NVDA closes at $178.67

The share price fell 4.27% from its November 6 close of $186.64. Reuters estimated a roughly $190 billion loss of market value for that session. The price change is observed; assigning the entire change to these allegations would be an unsupported causal inference.

Daily price record · Reuters context

REPORTED · Nov 9 · Public engagements

Planned appearances cancelled

TechCrunch reports Huang withdrew from the ElevenLabs Summit and other speaking engagements, including Anthropic's forum. A cancelled event is not a departure announcement.

TechCrunch report

LEGISLATIVE RISK · Nov 9 · Not enacted

Advanced-chip safeguards under renewed debate

Reuters identifies the AI Overwatch Act, Chip Security Act and Match Act as measures considered for FY2027 defense legislation. Debate and possible inclusion are not enacted law, and none proves a Megaspeed export violation.

Reuters legislative context

Separate evidentiary questions: Did an intelligence service attempt access? Was information compromised? Were GPUs unlawfully diverted? Were securities disclosures misleading? These cannot be collapsed into one allegation. Singapore's contrary statements and Nvidia's published denials remain part of the record. No public finding establishes knowing participation by Jensen Huang.

Update v2 · November 10, 2026. Original research cutoff November 8, 2026 retained in the independent GitHub evidence file. Evidence may change; no wrongdoing has been established.

Case status: OPEN. Intelligence reporting and potential chip-diversion concerns remain allegations. The underlying U.S. intelligence memorandum, transaction-level custody records and BIS licensing communications are not public in the material reviewed. The claim that anyone knowingly participated in espionage is not established.

Evidence question

Can the reported GPU imports, Nvidia's site checks, Megaspeed's financing and control, and actual permitted end users be independently reconciled? Did authorities receive actionable counterintelligence affecting any license decision, and if so what did they do?

FACT · Government action

Congress requests briefing

On November 5, committee chairs requested a private November 18 briefing; November 11 is the requested response deadline. Oversight is not a finding of misconduct.

House source

FACT · Singapore statement

Megaspeed inquiry ongoing

Singapore confirms its investigation into the company but denies conducting the reported Alice Huang intelligence inquiry.

Official correction

REPORTED · Intelligence allegation

Underlying memo unseen

FT and NYT describe an alleged recruitment and influence operation. Neither the memo nor supporting surveillance has been independently made public.

Attributed reporting

REPORTED · Inventory

Numbers require reconciliation

Bloomberg reports at least 136,000 imported GPUs and a smaller quantity identified in data-center checks. The categories are not equivalent.

Trade-data reporting

What remains unknown

  1. Megaspeed's ultimate economic owners, financing providers and relevant control arrangements.
  2. Unit-by-unit Nvidia GPU custody across customs entries, warehouses, data centers and authorized operators.
  3. Which export licenses were sought, issued, denied or not required under actual transaction facts.
  4. The recipients and contents of any dated U.S. intelligence warning or defensive briefing.
  5. The status, scope and outcome of any reported DOJ referral or future congressional briefing.

Contrary evidence and safeguards

Nvidia denies finding illegal diversion and calls the business contacts normal. Singapore disputes the claimed involvement of its intelligence agencies in an Alice Huang inquiry. Some overseas cloud computing services can be provided lawfully to customers who could not lawfully receive the same GPUs as hardware. None of those qualifications should be omitted when updating this case.

Do not link unrelated March 2026 U.S. diversion indictments to Megaspeed without evidence. Do not interpret an unresolved inventory gap as a physical smuggling finding. Public persons' private relationships are not evidence of an export violation by themselves.
