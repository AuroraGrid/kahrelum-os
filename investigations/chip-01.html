Investigations / CHIP-01 / OPEN

Nvidia, Megaspeed and the AI-chip custody question

An open, source-linked investigation into corporate control, GPU shipments, U.S. export rules and reported counterintelligence concerns.

Evidence reviewed November 8, 2026 · No finding of wrongdoing · Read the full Insight

Case status: OPEN. Intelligence reporting and potential chip-diversion concerns remain allegations. The underlying U.S. intelligence memorandum, transaction-level custody records and BIS licensing communications are not public in the material reviewed. The claim that anyone knowingly participated in espionage is not established.

Evidence question

Can the reported GPU imports, Nvidia's site checks, Megaspeed's financing and control, and actual permitted end users be independently reconciled? Did authorities receive actionable counterintelligence affecting any license decision, and if so what did they do?

FACT · Government action

Congress requests briefing

On November 5, committee chairs requested a private November 18 briefing; November 11 is the requested response deadline. Oversight is not a finding of misconduct.

House source

FACT · Singapore statement

Megaspeed inquiry ongoing

Singapore confirms its investigation into the company but denies conducting the reported Alice Huang intelligence inquiry.

Official correction

REPORTED · Intelligence allegation

Underlying memo unseen

FT and NYT describe an alleged recruitment and influence operation. Neither the memo nor supporting surveillance has been independently made public.

Attributed reporting

REPORTED · Inventory

Numbers require reconciliation

Bloomberg reports at least 136,000 imported GPUs and a smaller quantity identified in data-center checks. The categories are not equivalent.

Trade-data reporting

What remains unknown

  1. Megaspeed's ultimate economic owners, financing providers and relevant control arrangements.
  2. Unit-by-unit Nvidia GPU custody across customs entries, warehouses, data centers and authorized operators.
  3. Which export licenses were sought, issued, denied or not required under actual transaction facts.
  4. The recipients and contents of any dated U.S. intelligence warning or defensive briefing.
  5. The status, scope and outcome of any reported DOJ referral or future congressional briefing.

Contrary evidence and safeguards

Nvidia denies finding illegal diversion and calls the business contacts normal. Singapore disputes the claimed involvement of its intelligence agencies in an Alice Huang inquiry. Some overseas cloud computing services can be provided lawfully to customers who could not lawfully receive the same GPUs as hardware. None of those qualifications should be omitted when updating this case.

Do not link unrelated March 2026 U.S. diversion indictments to Megaspeed without evidence. Do not interpret an unresolved inventory gap as a physical smuggling finding. Public persons' private relationships are not evidence of an export violation by themselves.
