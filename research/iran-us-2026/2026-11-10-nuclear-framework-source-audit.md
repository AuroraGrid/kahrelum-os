## Core finding

There is direct and credible evidence that U.S.–Iran negotiations continue through Qatari mediation. The **specific alleged package**—Iranian domestic enrichment capped at 3.67%, treatment of its existing 60%-enriched uranium, release of $6–9 billion, reopening Hormuz and ending the U.S. naval blockade—**remains unverified and materially disputed**. A collection of outlets restating one anonymous-source Al Arabiya/Al Hadath report does not constitute multiple independent confirmations.

The 3.67% level corresponds to the enrichment ceiling in the 2015 JCPOA; that historical match does not prove U.S. or Iranian acceptance today. Likewise, a ceiling on **future production** does not dispose of an existing higher-enriched stockpile. Reports of a possible third-party role **do not establish that dilution, export, custody transfer, or IAEA verification has occurred**.

### F1: preliminary agreement — 45%

- **Question:** By **2026-11-30 23:59 UTC**, will both the U.S. government and Iran's government **publicly announce or sign the same negotiated preliminary nuclear agreement**, including at least one specific nuclear-enrichment constraint?
- **YES:** authoritative government statements from both sides identify the same preliminary agreement and its nuclear constraint, or an authenticated signed document is released and acknowledged by both.
- **NO:** deadline passes without that evidence. Anonymous reports, an unsigned proposal, a meeting, unilateral claims, or a ceasefire without agreed nuclear terms do not qualify.
- **Forecast:** **45% YES** (provisional analyst judgment; no independent model-run evidence).

### F2: operational reopening of Hormuz — 35%

- **Question:** By **2026-11-30 23:59 UTC**, will the Strait of Hormuz be **reopened for routine international commercial passage** under a public U.S.–Iran de-escalation arrangement?
- **YES:** both relevant restrictions are publicly lifted, and independent commercial/maritime reporting documents an uninterrupted **seven-day period** of routine international transits ending by the deadline.
- **NO:** public access still restricted, hostile closures persist, or fewer than seven corroborated uninterrupted days are documented. A tentative promise or a single tanker passage is insufficient.
- **Forecast:** **35% YES** (provisional analyst judgment).

### F3: independently verified high-enrichment drawdown — 25%

- **Question:** By **2026-12-31 23:59 UTC**, will the IAEA publicly report that it has **verified physical downblending of a nonzero, identified quantity** of Iran's uranium previously enriched to about 60% U-235 to a level no higher than 5%?
- **YES:** a dated IAEA public report or official statement confirms the inspected quantity, source enrichment and achieved enrichment level. **Partial** confirmed drawdown can qualify; complete disposal is **not** required by this narrow question.
- **NO:** no such IAEA verification is public by the deadline. Pledges, third-party statements, relocation alone, and uninspected dilution do not qualify.
- **Forecast:** **25% YES** (provisional analyst judgment).

## Adversarial review / disconfirming evidence

- A senior Qatari official was reported by Al Jazeera to reject alleged circulating deal terms. Crucial independent verification is still missing.
- November Reuters reporting still records major sequencing disputes and an **ongoing** U.S. maritime blockade.
- “3.67% agreed” could describe a floated negotiating option rather than a formally endorsed government position.
- “$6–9bn released” is a conditional discussion, not a money movement or sanctions order.
- “60% stockpile to be diluted” is an unresolved negotiation demand, not an inspected inventory or completed operation.
- Nuclear, sanctions, maritime and regional-war questions may be decoupled, allowing talks to continue without a complete package.
- Iran, Washington, Israel and regional stakeholders can each affect implementation and domestic acceptability.
