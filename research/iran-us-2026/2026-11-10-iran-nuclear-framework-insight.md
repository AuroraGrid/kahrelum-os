# Iran's 3.67% Nuclear Opening: The Negotiations Are Real. The Deal Is Not Yet Verified.

**KAHRELUM Insights | November 10, 2026 | Independent geopolitical analysis**

*The story is not that Tehran has surrendered its nuclear program. It is that an increasingly specific diplomatic bargain is circulating while the people who could authenticate it have not.* 

A dramatic report attributed to sources speaking to Al Arabiya/Al Hadath says the United States would accept uranium enrichment inside Iran up to **3.67%**, a limit associated with the 2015 nuclear agreement. In exchange, Tehran would reportedly address uranium enriched to **60%**, while a broader arrangement could release **$6 billion to $9 billion** in Iranian assets, reopen the **Strait of Hormuz**, and lift the U.S. maritime blockade.

If authenticated, this would be a serious diplomatic shift.

**But a proposal reported by anonymous sources is not the same as a signed nuclear deal.** A second newspaper repeating the same leak does not create a second independent source. And the most important contrary signal is inconvenient to those treating the headline as settled fact: a senior Qatari official was quoted by Al Jazeera on November 9 rejecting circulating reports about enrichment and asset-release terms as categorically false, while confirming that talks continue. That statement predates the later Al Arabiya report, so it should not be misrepresented as a direct denial of that specific later broadcast. It does, however, materially weaken confidence in unverified deal details.

## The real opening

Qatar's own government separately confirmed on November 7 that its mediators continued to coordinate with Washington and Tehran. Reuters reported on November 9 that President Donald Trump was holding off on fresh strikes for now while **the U.S. blockade remained in place**. Those are substantial, independently reported signals of diplomatic activity.

They do **not** prove either side has accepted the alleged 3.67% term. Nor do they establish any actual asset transfer, practical reopening of Hormuz, or inspection of enriched material.

This distinction is essential: a **diplomatic process** can be genuine even while the **specific deal text** circulating online is inaccurate.

## Why the 60% material matters more than the headline

A new cap on future enrichment and control of existing high-enriched material answer different technical questions. Tehran could agree in principle not to enrich *additional* uranium above 3.67% while the disposition of its previous 60%-enriched inventory remains unresolved.

Potential solutions include independently witnessed downblending or arrangements involving a third country. But words like “dilution,” “return of inspectors,” and “third-party mechanism” are **not evidence that anything has been physically removed, inspected, measured or downblended**. The International Atomic Energy Agency's access, inventory and verification authority are therefore central, not footnotes.

The same applies to the maritime exchange. Hormuz reopening is not a press-release metaphor. It would require observable commercial transits and lifting of relevant restrictions. A naval blockade cannot be described as lifted while the U.S. government says it remains in force.

## What would actually change the forecast?

Three kinds of evidence are decisive: matching public confirmation from Washington and Tehran; a verifiable IAEA inspection/downblending arrangement; and operational evidence that the blockade and shipping restrictions have ended.

Until then, the credible conclusion is narrower than the viral headline:

**Negotiations are active. A breakthrough is possible. The claimed 3.67% deal is disputed and not independently verified.**

That is not skepticism for its own sake. It is the difference between forecasting a diplomatic outcome and confusing a headline with the outcome itself.
