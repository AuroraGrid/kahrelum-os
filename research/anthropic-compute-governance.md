# Anthropic's compute commitments create a governance stress test

**KAHRELUM Research Dossier — October 2026**  
**Research question:** How should outsiders evaluate the governance pressure created when a frontier AI company simultaneously expands safety commitments and locks itself into enormous long-term compute capacity?

## Executive finding

Anthropic is pursuing two strategies at once: it is publicly expanding its safety-governance architecture while also committing to infrastructure at a scale that makes continued frontier development economically consequential.

The public record supports both sides of that statement.

Anthropic says its Responsible Scaling Policy (RSP) is meant to scale safeguards with model risk. In 2026 it added Frontier Safety Roadmaps, Risk Reports, public progress grading, and a separate Frontier Compliance Framework for legal obligations. At the same time, Anthropic announced more than $100 billion of AWS commitments over ten years, multiple gigawatts of additional Google/Broadcom capacity, and a large SpaceX compute agreement. Reuters later reported that Anthropic's confidential IPO filing described at least $518 billion of decade-long infrastructure commitments, with roughly 80% characterized as binding regardless of usage.

The evidence does **not** prove that infrastructure commitments have caused Anthropic to weaken a safety decision. It does establish a material governance question: the more capital is committed to frontier-scale growth, the higher the cost of slowing, pausing, or redirecting development if safety thresholds become binding.

That is a stress test worth monitoring.

## What the documents show

### FACT 1 — Anthropic has made very large, long-duration compute commitments

On April 20, 2026, Anthropic announced an agreement with Amazon for up to **5 gigawatts** of new compute and said it was committing **more than $100 billion over ten years** to AWS technologies.

Source: Anthropic, ["Anthropic and Amazon expand collaboration for up to 5 gigawatts of new compute"](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-amazon-compute).

Two weeks earlier, Anthropic announced a partnership with Google and Broadcom for multiple gigawatts of next-generation TPU capacity beginning in 2027, describing it as the company's most significant compute commitment to date at that point.

Source: Anthropic, ["Anthropic expands partnership with Google and Broadcom for multiple gigawatts of next-generation compute"](https://www.anthropic.com/news/google-broadcom-partnership-compute).

On May 6, Anthropic said it had also agreed to use capacity at SpaceX's Colossus 1 data center, adding more than 300 MW and over 220,000 Nvidia GPUs. The same announcement summarized other major commitments, including Amazon, Google/Broadcom, Microsoft/Nvidia, and Fluidstack.

Source: Anthropic, ["Higher usage limits and a SpaceX compute deal"](https://www.anthropic.com/news/higher-limits-spacex).

Reuters reported on September 29 that Anthropic's confidential IPO filing described **at least $518 billion** in infrastructure commitments over a decade and said approximately 80% were largely non-cancelable.

Secondary source: Reuters, ["Anthropic's $518 billion AI buildout hinges largely on deals that cannot be canceled, filing shows"](https://www.reuters.com/business/anthropics-518-billion-ai-buildout-hinges-largely-deals-that-cannot-be-canceled-2026-09-29/).

### FACT 2 — Anthropic simultaneously presents itself as a safety-governed frontier developer

Anthropic's Responsible Scaling Policy is designed to tie increasingly powerful systems to stronger mitigations. In February 2026 the company released RSP v3.0, separating company plans from broader industry recommendations and adding Frontier Safety Roadmaps and periodic Risk Reports.

Source: Anthropic, ["Responsible Scaling Policy Version 3.0"](https://www.anthropic.com/news/responsible-scaling-policy-v3).

Anthropic's current RSP page lists multiple revisions during 2026, including versions 3.1 through 3.4, and says Risk Reports are intended to give a candid view of model risk and preparedness.

Source: Anthropic, ["Anthropic's Responsible Scaling Policy"](https://www.anthropic.com/responsible-scaling-policy).

Anthropic also says governments, not companies alone, should set industry-wide rules with legal force and supports mandatory publication of catastrophic-risk evaluations and safety-testing summaries.

Source: Anthropic, ["AI policy"](https://www.anthropic.com/policy).

### FACT 3 — Not every safety goal is a hard commitment

Anthropic explicitly described its Frontier Safety Roadmap goals as ambitious and achievable but **not hard commitments**. It said these goals would instead be publicly graded.

Source: Anthropic, ["Responsible Scaling Policy Version 3.0"](https://www.anthropic.com/news/responsible-scaling-policy-v3).

One security roadmap item illustrates the distinction. Anthropic originally planned a phase of work on "provable inference" by May 15, 2026, then moved the target to September 30 after reallocating resources toward broader security work.

Source: Anthropic, ["Frontier Safety Roadmap Updates"](https://www.anthropic.com/responsible-scaling-policy/updates).

A changed date is not evidence of a safety failure. It does show that safety projects compete for finite organizational resources and can be reprioritized.

## The governance tension

### INFERENCE

Large non-cancelable compute commitments create an incentive structure that an outside evaluator should treat as relevant to safety governance.

If a company has committed hundreds of billions of dollars to infrastructure, the cost of materially slowing model development can rise. That does not mean executives will ignore safety thresholds. It means governance systems need to be strong enough to withstand commercial pressure if the two ever conflict.

This is an **incentive-risk hypothesis**, not an allegation of misconduct.

A useful analogy is capital-intensive finance: a large sunk or committed cost does not determine the next decision, but it changes the economic environment in which the next decision is made.

## Counterevidence

Several facts cut against a simplistic "growth overwhelms safety" narrative.

- Anthropic continues to publish RSP updates, risk reports, system cards, and a Transparency Hub.
- It publicly supports legal safety requirements rather than relying only on voluntary promises.
- In 2026 Anthropic said it temporarily redirected roughly 150 product engineers plus researchers toward security, reliability, privacy, and safeguards, while pausing most new feature development until exit criteria were met.
- Anthropic has implemented concrete weight-security controls and publishes more detail about its security program than many peers.

Source: Anthropic, ["Improving our alignment and security practices"](https://www.anthropic.com/news/improving-alignment-security-efforts).

These facts matter. A serious investigation has to preserve them.

## What would make the concern stronger?

The governance-risk hypothesis would strengthen if public evidence showed one or more of the following:

1. A safety threshold was crossed and a required mitigation was delayed primarily to protect deployment timelines or infrastructure economics.
2. A Risk Report, system card, or compliance document was materially weakened after a commercial conflict emerged.
3. Internal governance bodies objected to deployment and were overruled on commercial grounds.
4. Safety roadmap deadlines repeatedly moved while capacity-expansion milestones remained fixed.
5. Whistleblowers or formal records documented pressure to reinterpret risk thresholds to avoid slowing development.

None of those claims is established here.

## What would weaken the concern?

The hypothesis would weaken if Anthropic demonstrated that:

1. Governance bodies can pause or constrain frontier deployment even after large infrastructure commitments are signed.
2. Contracts contain flexibility that substantially limits the financial penalty of a safety-driven slowdown.
3. Safety decisions are insulated from revenue, infrastructure-utilization, or investor considerations.
4. Independent evaluators can verify that threshold-triggered mitigations are applied before deployment.

## Reporting questions

For Anthropic:

- What proportion of announced compute commitments can be deferred, reassigned, or canceled if a safety threshold requires slowing frontier deployment?
- Do RSP decision-makers receive information about the financial consequences of a pause? If so, how is that information governed?
- Has any deployment or training run been delayed in 2026 because an RSP or security requirement was not yet met?
- Which body has final authority when a safety recommendation conflicts with a major infrastructure commitment?
- Are Frontier Safety Roadmap delays reviewed for commercial-conflict risk?

For infrastructure partners:

- Do contracts include safety-triggered suspension or capacity-reallocation clauses?
- Can Anthropic preserve contracted capacity while delaying a frontier training run for safety reasons?

## Assessment

**FACT:** Anthropic has combined unusually large compute expansion with unusually elaborate public safety governance.

**INFERENCE:** The scale and rigidity of infrastructure commitments create a plausible governance pressure that merits external scrutiny.

**NOT ESTABLISHED:** That Anthropic has violated a safety commitment because of those obligations.

**Watch next:** evidence connecting a specific safety decision to commercial or infrastructure pressure.
