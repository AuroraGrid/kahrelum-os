Title: Anthropic’s $1.63 million lobbying trail: what it proves, and what it doesn’t

Subtitle: Original registrations document political connections. They do not establish that Anthropic engineered Jensen Huang’s departure.

An influence story can contain accurate dollar amounts and still reach beyond its evidence. That is the central problem with claims connecting Anthropic’s lobbying bills, Marco Rubio’s Washington network and Jensen Huang’s exit from Nvidia.

The numbers deserve scrutiny. The causal claim needs a different kind of proof.

### Start with what the records actually say

The federal lobbying register lists $740,000 in income reported by Continental Strategy for Anthropic from the second quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026. Ballard Partners reported another $890,000 across the first three quarters of 2026. The combined selected series is $1.63 million.

These are reported lobbying-income estimates, not audited bank transfers or personal payments to officials. They are also not Anthropic’s entire lobbying expenditure. Combining outside firms’ income with the client’s own expense reports can count overlapping spending twice.

The full quarter-by-quarter calculation and verification limits are in the [KAHRELUM evidence dossier](https://kahrelum.com/investigations/anthropic-lobbying-chip-policy).

### The Rubio connection is documented. Its meaning is limited.

Continental’s registration identifies Alberto Martinez’s former position as chief of staff to Senator Marco Rubio. Its first-quarter 2026 report lists an AI-development and national-security assignment and identifies Congress and the Executive Office of the President as contacted institutions.

That establishes a professional connection and declared lobbying activity. It does not identify a conversation about removing Huang, an instruction from Anthropic to do so, or an agreement to exchange money for that outcome.

Ballard’s registration makes another distinction easy to miss. It was effective March 9, 2026, although filed April 10. It names Brian Ballard, Sylvester Lukis and Micah Ketchel, whose disclosed background includes State Department and White House advisory roles. Its stated assignment is defense procurement. The entire fee cannot simply be relabeled chip-export lobbying.

### A policy fight is not proof of an executive-removal campaign

Anthropic’s public position on compute controls and Nvidia’s interest in overseas chip sales belong in this investigation. But a policy disagreement can reflect both security arguments and commercial interests. Neither, on its own, identifies who caused a board decision.

The original Federal Register supplies one firm historical anchor: effective January 15, 2026, qualifying H200 and equivalent exports to China and Macau moved to conditional, case-by-case license review. That was not unrestricted permission to sell. It also does not establish every subsequent policy change.

A separate Commerce–Justice statement says the Megaspeed investigation began in 2024. It describes a request for Nvidia records in May 2025 and an on-site check in June. That chronology predates both lobbying registrations in our selected series. It weighs against a claim that either engagement initiated that investigation, while leaving later-influence questions open.

### The missing link

To establish that Anthropic orchestrated Huang’s departure, we would need authenticated communications, instructions, meeting records or corroborated firsthand evidence connecting the client, lobbyists, officials and decision. We have not authenticated that evidence.

Former employment plus money plus timing is a reason to ask precise questions. It is not a substitute for their answers. A resignation, removal from a delegation and a board dismissal also must not be conflated.

Our dossier sets out competing explanations, the documents that could distinguish them, and the unresolved checks. It does not claim to have cleared every actor or disproved every possible influence effort. It identifies the boundary of the evidence available to this review.
