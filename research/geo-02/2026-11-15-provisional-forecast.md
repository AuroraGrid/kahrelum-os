### Scenario A: 30% — Direct U.S. kinetic strike on Iranian sovereign territory
- YES if the United States officially acknowledges, or at least two independent high-reliability outlets document, U.S. military-delivered kinetic weapons physically striking a target inside Iran's sovereign territory during the window.
- Counts even if new Gulf attacks also occur. Does not count U.S. interception over Bahrain, weapons on a U.S. vessel, Iran-claimed explosions without U.S. attribution, the existing blockade, cyber operations, or U.S. strikes solely in Yemen.
- Adjudication: CENTCOM / Pentagon statement, authenticated imagery with timing, Reuters/AP, and geographic location; contested actor => REVIEW, not automatic YES.

### Scenario B: 55% — Additional Iranian/Houthi Gulf attack without a U.S. strike on Iran
- Only if A is NO. YES if at least one *new*, independently time-stamped Iranian- or Houthi-attributed drone/missile/kinetic attack occurs within the window against U.S. personnel/equipment or Bahrain, Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Oman or Kuwait **and** a named local government or military authority publicly reports the event with a specific target country and dated incident. An interception still qualifies as an attempted kinetic attack.
- Mere warning sirens, social claims, unverified launch video, attacks elsewhere (e.g. in Yemen), and reporting published during this window about an *earlier* attack do not qualify. Attribution needs named government/official, not a channel aggregator.

### Scenario C: 15% — Neither defined event occurs
- No confirmed qualifying U.S. kinetic strike (A), and no qualifying new Gulf kinetic attack announcement tied to a post-issuance event time (B) by the resolution deadline.
