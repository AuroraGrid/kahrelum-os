# KAHRELUM | DoW FY2026 audit and AI evidence integrity
**Case ID:** KAH-ASSURANCE-DOW-FY26-001  
**Evidence cutoff:** 2026-11-17 (public sources only)  
**Status:** REVIEW-ONLY. This is a research/marketing demonstration, not an audit of the Department's systems, an investigation finding of wrongdoing, or evidence of an active procurement opportunity.

## Question
What can a third-party reviewer establish from public records about the Department of War's FY2026 agency-wide audit, its FY2026 $200 million AI/automation allocation, and the difference between an automated evidence trail and a trustworthy one?

## Supported observations
**E01 | November 16, 2026 department announcement.** The department stated clean component opinions *will* cover >56% of assets and >90% of liabilities, and explicitly included opinions expected in January or February 2027. It identified four expected opinions (Navy Working Capital Fund; DISA General Fund; DIA and NGA classified reports), alongside a clean Medicare-Eligible Retiree Health Care Fund opinion already received. This is a future-inclusive statement, not solely a count of signed opinions as of Nov. 16. Original: https://www.war.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/4649444/new-dow-audit-progress-marks-breakthrough-toward-a-department-wide-clean-opinion/

**E02 | Agency-wide result.** The FY2026 department-wide statements received a disclaimer of opinion for the ninth consecutive full-scope annual audit, according to contemporaneous IG-attributed reporting. A disclaimer means auditors could not obtain sufficient appropriate evidence for an opinion; it does not alone establish fraud or a missing asset amount. Reporting: https://breakingdefense.com/2026/11/pentagon-fails-9th-audit-asserts-path-towards-clean-books-in-2028/ ; https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/pentagon-fails-ninth-straight-audit-despite-progress-in-organizing-finances-5031201

**E03 | Reporting denominator / timing conflict.** Reuters quoted 14/25 standalone reporting entities passed, with 56% of assets and 86% of liabilities. Breaking Defense characterized 12 clean opinions already received and another four projected by February 2027; the department press release described >90% liabilities *including* future opinions. These are not safely interchangeable denominators, dates, or population definitions. Reconcile against signed component opinions and FY2026 financial section before making a definitive count. Sources: E01, E02.

**E04 | Funding plan, May 4, 2026.** The department's mandatory funding allocation plan lists $200 million in AI and automation for audit work: $110m transaction reconciliation; $30m evidential-matter gathering; $10m SOC 1 report expansion; $5m Advana software; $45m Navy AI/automation cases. It is an allocation plan, not proof all funds were obligated or that vendors are seeking bids. Primary document, page headed 54: https://comptroller.war.gov/Portals/45/Documents/news/FY2026_Mandatory_Funding_Allocation_Plan.pdf

**E05 | GAO's independent concern, May 13, 2026.** GAO said the new approach centers material account balances, large-sample/manual testing and AI where useful, rather than prioritizing fixing every underlying control deficiency. GAO welcomed progress but raised questions about fraud risk, material weaknesses, transparency and sustainability beyond 2028. Primary: https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-109115

**E06 | Positive counterevidence.** The department announced a SOC 1 Type 2 unmodified examination opinion for the War Data Platform for the stated Nov. 17, 2025–Jun. 30, 2026 period. A platform-specific controls examination is meaningful progress but is NOT an agency-wide clean financial statement opinion and is not blanket validation of AI models or their outputs. Department announcement: https://www.war.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/4591015/department-of-war-secures-unmodified-soc-1-type-2-opinion-for-war-data-platform/

**E07 | Consolidation is not remediation.** FY2026 press reporting attributes to the Inspector General the statement that the apparent decline in material weaknesses reflects consolidation, not correction. Do not treat a smaller headline count as independently resolved underlying issues. Source: https://justthenews.com/government/federal-agencies/pentagon-fails-financial-audit-ninth-consecutive-fiscal-year ; also GAO E05. Final detailed signed FY2026 OIG financial section requires direct document reconciliation.

## Claim disposition
- **SUPPORTED:** There is a $200m *planned allocation* for audit-related AI/automation; $30m line explicitly targets evidential-matter gathering.
- **SUPPORTED (reported, original audit PDF pending manual crosscheck):** FY2026 agency-wide disclaimer.
- **SUPPORTED:** The department projects more than 90% of liability coverage by February 2027 and explicitly includes future component opinions.
- **NOT PROVEN:** $200m has been fully obligated, contracts are available to small firms, or AI automation performed accurately.
- **NOT PROVEN:** Reduction in reported weaknesses means all underlying problems were fixed.
- **NOT PROVEN:** KAHRELUM, any named supplier, or any AI product contributed to an identified Pentagon discrepancy.
- **REJECTED AS INFERENCE:** The disclaimer itself proves missing trillions, theft or intent to deceive.

## Evidence that would change the assessment
1. The FY2026 AFR signed financial section and each component opinion, with report date and reporting entity defined.
2. The FY2026 OIG findings schedule and weakness-by-weakness reconciliation against FY2025.
3. Contract awards, obligations, modifications and actual test outcomes for the $200m allocation.
4. Independent output-level validation of generated audit evidence packets, exceptions, wrong matches, false positives and human escalations.
5. The full SOC 1 report scope, complementary user entity controls and limitations.
