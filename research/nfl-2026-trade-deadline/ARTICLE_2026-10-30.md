# NFL trade rumors have an evidence problem. Here's how KAHRELUM will test it.

**KAHRELUM Sports Intelligence Desk | Original editorial brief | October 30, 2026**

The NFL's October trade season is unusually active. The league's 2026 register records seven completed in-season deals by October 30, including Quenton Nelson, DeForest Buckner and Jeffery Simmons in a three-day span. That is the easy part of tracking trades: if an official transaction happened, a clean ledger can confirm it.

The harder part is everything that happens *before* a deal.

An insider reports that a team called. A beat reporter identifies a veteran who could move. A radio host thinks the fit makes sense. A source says a team wants a first-round pick. A sportsbook or prediction market shows a price. Each of those is a different kind of information. Merge them into one rumor and the audience loses the difference between fact, reporting and speculation.

Our public NFL Trade Intelligence Desk is built to keep those categories separate.

**Example one: Josh Downs.** ESPN's Jeremy Fowler reported an Indianapolis trade request on October 28. Downs' own first answer was noncommittal. On October 30, Colts coach Shane Steichen acknowledged that Downs had requested a move, confirming the central claim. Jordan Schultz separately attributed Pittsburgh interest to sources. Neither a confirmed request nor reported interest proves there is an accepted offer or an imminent transaction.

**Example two: Will McDonald IV.** Albert Breer's October 30 reporting describes interest from other clubs and resistance from the Jets. A meaningful inquiry can be real even while the odds of an actual deal remain uncertain. Source accuracy and trade probability are separate questions.

**Example three: players staying put.** Mike Garafolo reported on October 29 that multiple approaches for Colts defender Adetomiwa Adebawore met a refusal. That's more informative than an unsourced "he might be available" entry, even though it pushes the estimated trade probability *down*.

KAHRELUM is not publishing a fake real-time feed or ranking insiders by invented hit rates. Its first board is a dated, 124-record research snapshot. Many entries are explicitly analyst hypotheses, and only 20 have recovered provisional KAHRELUM probabilities. We will not present the recovered numbers as independently timestamp-verified forecasts. The official seven completed trades will not become retrospective success stories without valid pre-event evidence.

The test is simple: publish our actual probability, with its cutoff and strongest countercase, before the deadline. Preserve the record. When the NFL window closes at 4:00 p.m. New York time on November 10, score *every eligible candidate* against official transactions, including the many players who were not traded.

A forecasting system earns its reputation in its misses as much as its hits.
