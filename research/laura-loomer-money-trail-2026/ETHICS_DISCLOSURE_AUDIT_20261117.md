# KAHRELUM | Independent ethics and disclosure audit: paid research, distribution and political commentary
**Evidence cutoff:** November 17, 2026. **Status:** RESEARCH ADDENDUM / EDITORIAL HOLD. This is not a finding of unlawful conduct, knowingly false speech, or a verified purchased post. Do not merge or publish the held long-form allegation on the strength of this memo alone.

## Central question
What financial relationship should a reader know when a political commentator disseminates research or advocacy that may involve a paying client? The inquiry is analytically independent of whether a federal disclaimer law covers that post and independent of whether a specific statement is factually false.

A hidden material financial connection may affect how reasonable readers weigh the independence, selection, emphasis and completeness of a message, even if the writer genuinely holds the view and even if no provably false factual statement occurs. Conversely, a consulting payment alone does not show that the payee sold a political view, surrendered editorial control, or failed to disclose a specific post. These are distinct propositions.

## Four factual states that must not collapse
1. **Paid independent research:** A documented research fee with no client right to direct or compel publication. A conflict may be relevant for a writer covering the client or a related issue, but a paid post is not established.
2. **Paid research plus agreed distribution:** Evidence shows deliverables included promoting the report or findings to the creator's audience. This supports investigating a material sponsorship relationship, without proving viewpoint fabrication or contractual language control.
3. **Commissioned post or approved talking points:** A signed agreement, approved copy and dated public post establish paid political messaging for that post. Check whether a clear disclosure actually accompanied the specific publication.
4. **Knowingly false paid messaging:** Requires separate proof that a concrete factual claim was false or misleading and that the speaker knew or recklessly disregarded it. Not established by a consulting payment or by charge-per-post testimony.

The "paid opinion" concern is thus genuinely larger than a statutory violation, but allegations of deliberate lying or purchased opinions must not be asserted as findings without the additional proof.

## Reach, influence and the limits of audience-effect evidence
The Post reported approximately **1.9 million X followers** at the time of its November 7 article. That is nominal audience potential, not actual impressions, unique viewers, persuasion, a changed vote, or a White House policy outcome. One source said some clients *hoped* messages would be noticed at the White House; intent to reach officials is not proof of official action or of a direct pay-to-policy channel.

Pew's 2025 survey estimated that **21% of U.S. adults** regularly get news from social-media news influencers, with **38% of ages 18–29**. This describes the broader news ecosystem, not Loomer's audience. Source: https://www.pewresearch.org/journalism/fact-sheet/news-influencers-fact-sheet/

2026 peer-reviewed research surveyed **906 U.S. social-media users** and found disclosure support of bipartisan concern, with support positively associated with valuing authenticity and negatively associated with consuming more influencer-political content. This is survey evidence about transparency preferences, **not a causal Loomer audience study**. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1177/20563051261480944

A 2024 meta-analysis of 37 influencer-marketing studies, 12,721 participants, found that sponsorship disclosure increased advertising recognition and reduced source credibility on average; this is largely commercial marketing evidence, **not proof of political persuasion or falsehood in this case**. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11002-024-09757-z

## Ethical framework and narrower legal boundary
The Society of Professional Journalists' voluntary code urges reporters to disclose unavoidable conflicts, resist favorable treatment for sponsors, distinguish advertising from reporting and label sponsored material. These principles are useful standards for examining a public-facing commentator who invokes journalism, but SPJ is **not a regulator or legally enforceable rule**. https://www.spj.org/spj-code-of-ethics/

The FTC's own endorsement guidance expressly says it **does not have jurisdiction over political advertisements**. Commercial-product influencer rules must not be presented as an automatic legal requirement for every political post. https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking

The FEC's 2024 internet public-communication framework distinguishes paying an advertising platform to boost content from merely paying a person to create/share content with their own followers. This does not immunize all conduct from every federal or state rule; the actual payer, activity, content, platform boosting and jurisdiction still matter. Contemporaneous FEC statement: https://www.fec.gov/resources/cms-content/documents/Statement-re-Tech-Mod-Final-Rule-12-14-23.pdf

The Campaign Legal Center petitioned the FEC in October 2025 for stronger paid-influencer disclosure rules. It treats the lack of audience-facing identification of the payer as a transparency concern even when the underlying content is not independently shown false. Petition landing page: https://campaignlegal.org/document/clc-petitions-fec-require-disclaimers-influencers-paid-political-ads

The ethical conflict-of-interest test is: **Would a reasonable reader weigh this message differently if they knew the particular payer, topic, terms and editorial controls?** It is not: **Was the commentator paid at any time?** Payment unrelated to the content is not enough for a categorical sponsored-post finding.

## Evidence questions that can actually resolve the distinction
- Seek redacted scope-of-work, invoice, deliverable acceptance, editorial-control terms, distribution instructions and payment settlement for each allegedly sponsored publication; protect private banking identifiers and unrelated client secrets.
- Request exact public X post IDs, created/edited times and any visible sponsorship markers from those asserting direct payment. Do not infer posts merely because their wording resembles client positions.
- Ask Loomer to specify whether any content she shared covered work funded by clients, and whether she has a standing or post-specific financial-relationship disclosure policy.
- Ask Bruesewitz whether he can furnish redacted agreement language and a nonprivate post linkage supporting his distinction between research and paid tweets, and whether the publishing client reviewed/approved the final words.
- Ask America One for the scope of **its own 2026** "RESEARCH CONSULTING" disbursements and whether they required dissemination. Do not presume these match the **2025** X Strategies account.
- Distinguish an opinion from checkable factual statements. For identified posts, separately evaluate claims against dated primary records, sourcing, edits and counterevidence. Without an authenticated post corpus, no aggregate "false political opinions" verdict is supportable.
- Evaluate actual exposure (post views, reposts) if authenticated; even high impressions are not evidence of conversion, vote change or policy effect.

## Editorial posture (still HOLD)
A source-grounded investigation can report **filed consulting payments, conflicting descriptions of research versus posting, and the public-interest significance of potential undisclosed sponsorship**. It cannot yet state as fact that particular opinions were bought, posts were intentionally false, an unlawful scheme existed or official decisions resulted.

Suggested evidence-led working headline: **"Political Commentary or Commissioned Distribution? What the Public Payment Records Do, and Do Not, Establish."** This is a draft headline, not publication approval.
