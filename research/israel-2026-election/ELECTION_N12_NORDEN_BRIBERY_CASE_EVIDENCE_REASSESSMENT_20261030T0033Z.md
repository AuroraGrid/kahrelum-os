## FACT: what has actually been published

It does **not** prove Norden fabricated information, ordered arrests, broke a law, or initiated the later four arrests.
- **Prime Minister's Office denial:** N12 expressly states at the end of its article that the Prime Minister's Office **denied the report**. This denial is an essential opposing claim and is preserved; the identity of the initial informer remains **CONTESTED**.
- **Anonymous law-enforcement appraisal quoted by N12:** Senior enforcement officials reportedly believe the case against those first three suspects is weak and unlikely to yield an indictment, and say the suspects gave an account of the money's origin. This is a provisional assessment, **NOT a prosecutorial dismissal, closure, exoneration, court ruling or certainty of no future indictment**.
- **Specific correction:** N12 says reports that fuel vouchers were found in suspects' possession are incorrect. This corrects a specific alleged seizure, **not** the distinct police-attributed claim concerning cash in envelopes. No verified itemized signed evidence inventory was retrieved.
- **Distinct cases:** N12 attributes to a police source that the three-person Ra'am bribery case is **unconnected** to the separate Shin Bet foreign-funding investigation. Shin Bet says its own election-influence intelligence did not originate from political parties. The separate Shin Bet case is not disposed of by the N12 three-person case report.
- Ra'am disputes the original suspicions, while its critics call for investigation. Police reported a total of seven arrests across the October 27 and 29 investigations, but judicial resolution remains unknown.
